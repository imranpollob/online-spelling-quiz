# Commonly Misspelled English Words: Evidence-Based Word Bank

**Prepared:** 20 October 2026  
**Scope:** General English spelling; international English learners and English-language writing  
**Purpose:** Prioritize words for a spelling quiz and provide a broader, sourced word collection.

## Executive summary

There is no defensible single ranking of the most misspelled English words in the world. Available sources measure different populations and behaviors: international exam-taking learners, school pupils in England, dictionary lookups in the United States, learner exam corpora in the Middle East and North Africa, or words editors consider difficult. These should not be collapsed into one precise global league table.

The strongest global learner-corpus signal is the set **because, which, accommodation, advertisement, beautiful**. Cambridge reports these as the words its Cambridge English Corpus analysis found learners worldwide misspelled most often. Other repeated candidates across corpus, dictionary, exam, and editorial lists include **definitely, separate, environment, beginning, address, receive, until, embarrassing, recommend, business, believe, grateful,** and **occurrence**. [S1–S5]

The tiers below are an evidence synthesis, not measured global ranks. Tier 1 has direct international learner-corpus support and/or strong recurrence across independent source types. Tier 2 recurs in several regional, exam, dictionary, or editorial lists. Tier 3 is a useful wider practice set; its exact frequency is less certain or the word is difficult for a narrower learner group. All 20 Tier 1 target words are already present in this project’s 835-word built-in library.

## How to read the tiers

- **Tier 1 — highest-priority, broad evidence:** strongest candidates for frequent quiz rotation.
- **Tier 2 — frequently reported:** recurring across multiple useful lists or learner samples, but less consistently ranked.
- **Tier 3 — wider practice bank:** often difficult or reported as misspelled, though not established as globally frequent.
- Incorrect forms are examples, not exhaustive inventories or frequency rankings. A form can be common in one region or learner group and rare in another.
- Words such as *their/there* are real words with different meanings. They are contextual word-choice errors, not misspellings of the individual words.

## Context-dependent errors: teach with a sentence

These are common errors in writing but are not ordinary misspellings of the individual words. The forms on both sides are valid English spellings, so an audio-only single-word quiz cannot reliably distinguish them.

| Intended distinction | Typical confusion | Quiz treatment |
|---|---|---|
| there / their / they're | *there* for *their* | Use a sentence and test meaning/usage |
| your / you're | *your* for *you're* | Use a sentence and test meaning/usage |
| to / too / two | *to* for *too* or *two* | Use a sentence and test meaning/usage |
| were / where | exchanged forms | Use a sentence; both are real words |
| woman / women | *women* for *woman* | Use a sentence; Cambridge GCSE sample found this repeatedly [S9] |
| knew / new | exchanged forms | Use a sentence; Cambridge GCSE sample found this repeatedly [S9] |
| could have | *could of* | Teach as a grammar/usage error, not a misspelling [S5] |
| a lot | *alot* | Teach spacing as a word-boundary error [S5] |

Cambridge Assessment’s GCSE study is a useful real-script control, but it is a small, historical UK sample: researchers identified 345 errors in 11,730 words from 2004 exam scripts. In that sample, the recurrent words included *woman/women, were/where, there/their, knew/new, too,* and *until*. Those frequencies should not be generalized to all ages or countries. [S9]

## Regional spellings: do not score a valid variant as an error

Choose a target locale for the quiz, or accept all listed variants. Examples include:

- **acknowledgment / acknowledgement** and **judgment / judgement** (American / British usage in the cited Dictionary.com guidance). [S4]
- **license / licence** (American noun and verb spelling *license*; British noun *licence*, verb *license*). [S4]
- **center / centre**, **program / programme**, and **maneuver / manoeuvre** (regional conventions). [S5, S8]
- **canceled / cancelled**, **gray / grey**, and other accepted variants should follow an explicitly chosen dictionary/locale.

## Recommendation for a spelling quiz

1. Rotate Tier 1 most often; use Tier 2 for regular review and Tier 3 for challenge rounds.
2. Save a correct spelling, one or more error examples, a short explanation, and a locale tag for each entry.
3. Give homophones and real-word confusions sentence context. Do not use isolated audio for pairs pronounced identically.
4. Set the quiz’s regional English convention and accept valid variants for that convention.
5. Treat the tiers as a practical learning priority, then refine them with the app’s own anonymous miss-response counts. Do not label the order a worldwide ranking.

## Interpretation and limitations

A dictionary lookup, a search for “how to spell,” and an observed typo in an essay are different events. They can all identify words people find difficult, but their counts cannot be compared directly. Exam corpora also represent people who took a particular exam and may overrepresent specific ages, proficiency levels, first languages, and writing tasks. The Cambridge CLC provides the best international learner evidence found here, but it is not a probability sample of every English user worldwide. The report therefore gives evidence tiers and source-specific claims, not fabricated global percentages or an exact universal rank.
